At Dymension, I worked at the intersection of product strategy, research, and engineering to help turn a modular blockchain architecture into an end-to-end platform for launching and operating rollups.

## The opportunity

Dymension began with a different vision for modular blockchains. Rather than requiring sovereign rollups to establish trust and communicate directly with one another, it proposed a shared settlement layer to coordinate security, bridging, and interoperability. Rollups—individual blockchains that rely on other infrastructure for parts of their operation—could connect through this shared layer.

The architecture addressed important technical challenges, but it left a product question unanswered: **How would developers launch rollups, raise capital, and attract users without assembling an entire ecosystem of external services?**

## Shaping the product

When I joined Dymension, I helped evolve the project from a settlement infrastructure protocol into a more complete rollup launch platform.

One of my most consequential contributions was advocating for a native automated market maker (AMM) at the settlement layer. An AMM provides a way to trade tokens through liquidity pools. My reasoning was straightforward: rollups needed immediate access to liquidity, a way to establish markets for their tokens, and a unified experience for developers and users.

I proposed incorporating the AMM into the protocol's architecture, convinced leadership to adopt the direction, and helped integrate the concept into the project's white paper.

During implementation, I also advocated for a different underlying software architecture. I pushed for an alternative to the originally proposed fork that would better support future integrations and the capabilities we ultimately wanted to deliver.

Together, these decisions helped shape Dymension's evolution toward a rollup launch platform with integrated liquidity and bridging.

## Leading across stakeholders

A significant part of my role involved aligning people around a complex technical vision. I worked across leadership, engineering, and the broader community, translating product strategy into technical priorities and communicating architectural decisions and their implications.

I also authored much of the project's public-facing material, including technical articles, product explanations, community messaging, and social content.

The challenge was helping different audiences understand why the architecture mattered and what it would enable.

## Outcomes and reflections

Dymension raised approximately $7 million across its early funding rounds and attracted significant attention during the growth of the modular blockchain ecosystem.

We developed the platform beyond its original infrastructure thesis, introducing integrated liquidity and evolving toward a more complete rollup launch experience.

Despite strong initial interest, the ecosystem struggled to develop sustained adoption. Market attention moved faster than we could establish durable traction.

That experience reinforced an important distinction for me: **a compelling technical architecture does not automatically translate into a successful product.**

I learned to look beyond what a system can enable and think critically about the tradeoffs each architectural and product decision introduces—particularly how those decisions affect the long-term product vision.

It's a principle I continue to apply: understand the system, anticipate its constraints, and make decisions that serve the product's ultimate purpose.
